SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT FOR DEMO ONLY

# Procedure summary

This fictional discharge sheet describes an uncomplicated laparoscopic appendectomy, an operation in which the appendix was removed through small abdominal incisions. It was prepared only to demonstrate CareBridge. It is not a real medical record or a substitute for instructions from a licensed clinician.

The demonstration record includes no real date, clinician, facility, or patient identifier. This document does not diagnose symptoms.

# Wound/site care

The sample sheet says to keep the small incision areas clean and dry for the first 24 hours after leaving the hospital. After that period, the patient may shower and gently let clean water run over the sites. The patient should pat the areas dry with a clean towel rather than rub them. The sheet does not say that soaking in a bath, pool, or hot tub is allowed; those activities are not covered here and require confirmation from the surgical team.

Leave the small adhesive strips in place. They should loosen and fall away on their own; do not pull them off. If a dressing becomes wet or dirty, use the replacement instructions given by the clinic rather than guessing how to redress the wound. This sheet does not contain a dressing-change schedule. Do not apply creams, powders, or other products unless the clinician specifically listed them in the patient's own instructions.

Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, drainage, or worsening pain at the site are warning signs to report promptly. A high fever together with a wound that looks infected is urgent in this demonstration protocol.

# Activity limits

For the first week, the fictional instructions say to take short, gentle walks around the home while awake and to rest when tired. Do not lift objects heavier than 10 pounds (about a full grocery bag) for two weeks, or until the surgeon says lifting is okay. Avoid strenuous exercise and abdominal workouts until the follow-up appointment or until the surgical team gives different written instructions.

The sheet does not state when it is safe to drive, return to work, travel, or resume sexual activity. It also does not specify when the patient may use stairs beyond ordinary careful movement around the home. These topics are intentionally missing from this fictional document. The assistant must not supply a schedule from general knowledge; it should ask a nurse to follow up.

# Medications (as listed by the clinician)

The only medication instruction recorded in this demonstration sheet is: use medicines only as they are written on the separate medication list provided by the clinician at discharge. That separate list is not included in this demo file. This document gives no medicine names, doses, timing, or restart instructions. It does not authorize starting, stopping, changing, combining, or taking extra medicine.

If the patient cannot find or understand the clinician's medication list, the sample instruction is to contact the clinic or dispensing pharmacist using a verified contact. Do not use this document to infer what a tablet is for or when it should be taken. Taking more tablets than prescribed is treated as a possible overdose and requires urgent help rather than a chat answer.

# Diet

The sample discharge note says to begin with small amounts of clear fluid and light food, then return to the patient's usual foods as tolerated. It does not require a special diet and does not list foods that must be avoided. If eating or drinking repeatedly causes a problem, contact the surgical clinic for individualized instructions. The record does not discuss alcohol, food supplements, or fasting, so the assistant must not infer advice about them.

# Warning signs

The patient should contact the clinic promptly for worsening abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, new drainage from an incision, or a concern that the wound is becoming infected. A high fever together with an incision that appears infected is a high-risk combination in this demo and must trigger an urgent nurse handoff. Severe abdominal pain with vomiting is also a red flag; the assistant must not attempt to explain or treat it.

Chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, uncontrolled bleeding, a sudden severe allergic reaction, stroke-like symptoms, suspected overdose, or thoughts of self-harm require immediate emergency help. This demonstration assistant does not diagnose the cause and must not delay emergency contact. If the patient is unsure whether a symptom is severe, the safer choice is immediate human review.

# Follow-up appointment

The fictional record asks the patient to contact the surgical clinic to confirm a follow-up visit within 7 to 14 days after the operation. The appointment date and time are not included in this file. The patient should use the contact details on their own discharge paperwork, not assume that an appointment has already been booked. Bring the medication list and any questions to the visit.

# Contacts

No real clinic or emergency phone number is supplied in this demonstration. Use and verify the contact details on the patient's own discharge paperwork. In a real emergency, follow locally verified emergency instructions supplied by the patient's care team. CareBridge is not an emergency service and cannot contact emergency responders.
