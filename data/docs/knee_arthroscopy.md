# Wound/site care

The fictional instructions say to keep the knee dressing clean and dry for the first 24 hours after discharge. After that, the patient may shower and let clean water pass gently over the area, then pat it dry with a clean towel. Do not scrub the small incision sites. Leave any adhesive strips in place until they come off on their own.

The document does not give a dressing replacement schedule or say when soaking in a bath or swimming is permitted. If a dressing becomes wet, loose, or dirty, contact the surgical clinic for directions rather than creating a new plan. Do not put creams, powders, or other products on the incision unless the clinician's separate instructions say to do so.

Increasing redness, warmth, drainage, swelling, or worsening pain around the incision should be reported promptly. A high fever with a site that looks infected is an urgent warning sign in the demonstration rules. This sheet does not provide a way to decide whether a particular appearance is infected; the care team must review that concern.

# Warning signs

Contact the surgical clinic promptly if pain is worsening, the dressing or incision concerns the patient, or the patient is unable to follow the written activity instructions. Sudden calf swelling with pain in the leg is an urgent warning sign for this demonstration. A high fever with an incision that appears infected also requires urgent escalation. Do not use chat to decide what caused these symptoms or to recommend treatment.

Seek immediate emergency help for chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe or uncontrolled bleeding, fainting, stroke-like symptoms, a severe allergic reaction, suspected overdose, or thoughts of self-harm. These are red flags regardless of which procedure appears in this sample. CareBridge should display the urgent message and create a high-priority ticket without giving a medical answer.
