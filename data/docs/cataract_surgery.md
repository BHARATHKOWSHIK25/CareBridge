# Procedure summary

This fictional discharge sheet describes cataract surgery on one eye. In this sample procedure, the cloudy natural lens was removed and a clear artificial lens was placed. This record exists only for software testing. It contains invented details, is not a real patient's record, and is not a substitute for advice from an eye-care professional. Nothing in this file should be treated as a diagnosis or as an instruction for an actual patient.

The demonstration record has no real surgery date, clinician name, facility, or identifying information. The care team is represented by a fictional clinic contact at the end of the sheet. A patient's own written discharge paperwork takes priority over this sample. If a question is not answered explicitly in this file, the safe response is to say the file does not cover it and arrange human follow-up rather than filling in a likely-sounding answer.

# Wound/site care

The sample instructions say to use the protective eye shield while sleeping for the first seven nights after surgery. During waking hours, wear ordinary glasses if they are comfortable and available. Do not press or rub the operated eye. Wash hands before touching the area around the eye or handling a prescribed eye-drop bottle.

This file does not give instructions for washing the face, using cosmetics, swimming, bathing, or cleaning the eyelids. It does not describe which mild sensations are expected, and the assistant must not decide whether a symptom is normal. New or worsening discomfort should be discussed with the eye clinic. Sudden loss of vision is an urgent warning sign and must cause an immediate handoff, not a generated answer.

# Warning signs

Sudden loss of vision in either eye, severe eye pain, significant new swelling, or a sudden change after the operation should be reported immediately to the eye clinic or emergency service according to the patient's local instructions. Sudden vision loss is a red flag in this demo: CareBridge must show only an urgent message and create an urgent nurse ticket. It must not ask the patient to wait for a routine appointment or try to identify the cause.

Chest pain, difficulty breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, fainting, stroke-like symptoms, severe allergic reaction, possible overdose, or thoughts of self-harm are also urgent. The sample assistant does not diagnose or treat these symptoms. If the patient feels unsafe or is uncertain about an emergency, they should contact verified local emergency services directly.
